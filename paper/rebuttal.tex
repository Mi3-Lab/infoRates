We thank all reviewers. \R{2} and \R{3} question whether our protocol measures
temporal aliasing. \textbf{We investigated and they are correct.} We can now
state the mechanism exactly, and the corrected analysis is more informative than
the original claim. We report it here rather than defend the original framing.

\head{What the protocol actually varies.}
\texttt{select\_frame\_indices} forms \texttt{candidates\,=\,arange(0,\,window,\,stride)}
and then re-uniformises them to the model's frame budget with \texttt{linspace}.
At fixed coverage the selected frames therefore span the same window at
\emph{every} stride; stride changes the input only once
$\lceil\text{window}/s\rceil < \text{budget}$, and the deficit is filled by
\emph{repeating the last frame}. In physical units (median seconds between
sampled frames, cov$=$100\%):

UCF-101 & 0.395 & 0.395 & 0.395 & 0.395 & 0.617 \\
AUTSL   & 0.125 & 0.125 & 0.131 & 0.252 & 0.475 \\

Four of five columns are the same sampling rate. Three measurements follow.
(i) Restricted to clips that never pad, the stride $1\!\to\!16$ drop collapses
from $+10.7$pp to $-0.5$pp, in \textbf{21 of 21} model--dataset pairs with a
usable unpadded subset; on AUTSL, SSv2 and DriveAct no clip escapes padding.
(ii) Spearman(padding rate, \textsc{TDS})$=+0.964$ --- yet among the three
fully-saturated datasets \textsc{TDS} still spans 21.9--53.0pp, a 31pp residual
padding cannot explain. (iii) Spearman(frame budget, published stride
drop)$=+0.849$, $p{=}0.008$: the two ``robust'' models are exactly the two with
budget$=8$.

\head{What survives.}
Supplying every architecture the \emph{same $k$ distinct frames at the same
positions}, accuracy at matched evidence still tracks the published robustness
ordering (Spearman $+0.821$, $p{=}0.023$, over the 7 datasets the published
ordering was computed on). We flag that this test is panel-sensitive: adding
Kinetics-400, which we evaluate with pretrained rather than fine-tuned
backbones, moves it to $+0.571$, $p{=}0.18$. With only 7 architectures the
statistic is fragile either way, and we report both. The ordering is
directionally consistent across every $k$ we tested; the $3\text{--}5\times$
magnitude was the budget artifact. We rewrite C2 as
\emph{``at equal evidence budget, divided-attention and state-space designs
% [CUT-7] inconclusive by design; supplementary carries it
deliver more accuracy per distinct frame''} and drop the magnitude claim. We also ran two further tests. \emph{(a)} With $k$ pinned to each model's budget and only the window width varying --- so the sampling rate changes tenfold while the frame count does not --- accuracy \emph{rises} with a wider window in 7 of 8 datasets (Spearman $+1.0$ in four of them, up to $+55.6$pp). If undersampling were the dominant cost the sign would reverse; over the range this protocol reaches, the binding constraint is how much of the action is seen. \emph{(b)} An anti-aliasing intervention (same $k$ and span, point-sampled vs.\ temporally box-filtered) never recovers accuracy. We deliberately do \emph{not} read this as evidence against aliasing: our models were fine-tuned on sharp frames, so filtered input is out-of-distribution and would cost accuracy regardless. Only a gain would have been informative. We report it, and its inconclusiveness, in the supplementary.

\head{\R{3} (vdwp).}
\emph{Frame rate.} Correct, and worse than stated: the fps table is hardcoded
and wrong for three datasets (HMDB-51 is 30 not 25, AUTSL 30 not 25, EPIC 50 not
60; FineGym is absent and defaults to 25). At fixed nominal stride the real
interval varies $3.9\times$ across datasets. We re-express every temporal axis in
seconds. \emph{Kinetics-400.} Added with no training --- all architectures are
K400-pretrained and we verified label-order identity. It sits at the low-demand
end, which sharpens the paper's point: standard recipes were tuned on the
benchmark where sampling matters least. \emph{Table 5.} Regenerated for all 8
datasets from one pool, FineGym included. \emph{Table 7.} Your suspicion is
confirmed: fine-tuning at the \emph{native} resolution already gains $+6$ to
$+10.6$pp for 7 of 8 models, so the $+39.2$pp is $\approx$32pp resolution
adaptation plus $\approx$7pp extra training. We report both. Model ordering will
be made consistent.

\head{\R{1} (XdvJ).}
\emph{Frames / span / interval versus stride.} Answered by the mechanism above;
% [CUT-8]
it now appears explicitly in Sec.~3.2. \emph{Multi-stride augmentation.} Run, with two arms (augmenting through the published sampler, and with uniform resampling) against the published checkpoint. \textbf{You are substantially right, more than we expected.} Exposure recovers $28.4\%$ of the cliff for TimeSformer and $37.0\%$ for R3D-18, whose stride-16 accuracy rises $6.5\%\!\to\!31.9\%$. Two caveats: 63--72\% of the cliff persists, and the augmentation costs $3.3$/$9.9$pp of dense accuracy, so it redistributes rather than repairs. Most diagnostically, at matched dense accuracy the arm trained \emph{through} the padding recovers $28.4\%$ versus $11.7\%$ for uniform resampling --- learning to cope with the frozen tail is worth $2.4\times$ more than learning multi-rate sampling, which is what one expects if the cliff is an input artefact rather than a temporal-frequency effect. \emph{Convergence at 48/96px.} Extracted from 730 training logs: at
48px \textbf{52.8\%} of runs still peak at the final epoch (16--27\% elsewhere),
so the budget does bind there; but the accuracy gained in the last three epochs
is 1.38pp, the smallest of any resolution. We state this as a limitation.
\emph{Table 7 baseline column, Sec.~3.3 PE interpolation, S19 SSv2 discussion:}
all added.

\head{\R{2} (pxtb).}
\emph{Routing.} Both points correct. The rule thresholds $\max_c p(c|v)$, so
``entropy routing'' is a misnomer and is renamed. With $\tau$ chosen on a
calibration half and scored on the held-out half (1000 stratified splits), the
headline SSv2 cell moves from $+0.21$pp to $\mathbf{-0.03}$pp, 95\% CI
$[-1.07,+0.83]$; across 56 pairs the mean gain over fixed dense inference is
$-3.5$pp. The abstract's ``halves temporal budgets without accuracy loss'' and
the title's ``Mitigating'' are removed. \emph{Statistics.} Correct that the
published ANOVA treats 25 cells scoring the same clips as independent. Re-run as
a within-subject design (clip $=$ subject, each effect against its own
subject$\times$factor error), partial $\eta^2$ is coverage $0.292$, stride
$0.232$, interaction $0.070$; ``coverage is king at $2.2\times$'' becomes
$1.26\times$, while the per-model stride ordering is unchanged. The
stride$\times$coverage interaction \emph{is} reported (S13, FineGym 15.0\% of
grid variance); we accept it was neither in the ANOVA model nor beyond two
% [CUT-6]
datasets, and now give all 56 pairs. \emph{\textsc{TDS} is weak.} Each
sub-objection is testable and none changes the ranking: dropping $[\cdot]^+$ is
numerically identical (it never fires), the curve integral gives Spearman
$+1.000$, baseline- and chance-normalised variants give $+0.952$.
\emph{Hardware scaling.} Correct --- the $\times1.5/\times10/\times50$ factors
were estimates, and are removed.

\head{Disclosed limitations.}
The Table 5 thresholds as printed are not reproducible from our artifacts
(relative-drop definitions match 3 of 7 datasets, suggesting a units mismatch);
we regenerate the table and state the definition. VideoMamba is absent from the
matched-evidence analysis due to a CUDA build incompatibility, and FineGym could
not be re-evaluated; both are stated rather than glossed.
